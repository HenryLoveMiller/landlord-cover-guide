Landlord insurance FAQ

The questions landlords actually ask, answered from published research and standard policy wording.

Is landlord insurance compulsory in Australia?
No. There is no legal requirement to hold it. Your mortgage lender will almost certainly require it as a condition of the investment property loan, so in practice it is compulsory for anyone with a rented-out loan.
What is the average cost of landlord insurance?
According to Canstar Research (March 2026), the national average annual premium is $2,640 for a house and $432 for a unit, on a $1,000 excess.
Which state has the most expensive landlord insurance?
North Queensland at $4,482 a year for a house, then the Northern Territory at $4,157. Both are driven by cyclone and flood exposure.
Why is it cheaper to insure a unit than a house?
The body corporate normally insures the building, so the landlord policy only has to cover contents and rental exposure. That is why a Queensland unit averages $396 against $2,529 for a house.
What does an unoccupied excess mean?
If the property is empty for more than a set period (often 30 or 60 consecutive days), claims are subject to a much higher excess — commonly $1,000 on top of the base excess. Some insurers apply a percentage of the sum insured instead. Check the PDS.
Can I claim landlord insurance as a tax deduction?
You may be able to, in certain circumstances. Whether your premiums qualify is a question for a registered tax agent, not for this site.
Does raising my excess actually save money?
Yes on the premium — but the trade-off is what you pay per claim. On a house averaging $2,640 a year, a $500 excess may save tens of dollars a year. If you have a $50,000 claim that is a poor trade. Match the excess to your cash position, not to the saving.
Should I insure the building or just the contents?
If you own the property, insure the building — if you do not, a total loss leaves you with nothing but the contents claim. Most landlords insure building plus contents, which is also usually cheaper than two separate policies.
Sources. Canstar Research, “Home Insurance: What does landlord insurance cost in Australia?” published 17 March 2026 (https://www.canstar.com.au/home-insurance/the-cost-of-landlord-insurance/).
