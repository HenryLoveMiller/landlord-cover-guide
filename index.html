Units are far cheaper because the body corporate normally insures the building; the landlord policy covers the contents and the rental exposure.

Two figures that catch people out

North Queensland costs $4,482 a year for a house — 70% above the national average. Cyclone and monsoon exposure are priced separately from the rest of Queensland.

Units can be a fraction of the cost. A Queensland strata unit averages $396 against $2,529 for a house in the same state.

What changes your premium

  • What you cover. Building only, contents only (carpets, appliances), or both. Building and contents together is usually cheaper than buying them separately.
  • Sum insured. The higher the declared value of the building and contents, the higher the premium.
  • Property characteristics. Build material, roof type, age and condition. Old or unusual materials raise the premium.
  • Location. Postcode matters. Areas prone to cyclone, flood, bushfire or high crime cost more.
  • Optional cover. Loss of rent if a tenant defaults or breaks the lease, accidental damage, tenant pet damage — each adds to the premium.
  • Security features. Home alarms, security screens and monitored systems can reduce the premium.
  • Excess. Raising your excess lowers the premium, but you pay more at claim time. A $1,000 excess is the basis for every figure on this site.
  • Claims history. A history of claims raises the premium at renewal.
  • Number of properties. Insuring multiple properties with one insurer is often cheaper.

Why landlord cover is cheaper than home cover

Landlord insurance is generally cheaper than home and contents insurance because the owner is not living in the property. Canstar puts the national house average at $2,640 against $2,795 for home and contents cover.

Premiums across the industry have been rising, with extreme weather accounting for $1.8 billion in insured costs in a single year (Insurance Council of Australia data, cited by Canstar).

Five ways to lower it

  • Bundle building and contents with one insurer.
  • Raise the excess — but understand you pay more at claim time.
  • Install alarms, security screens and monitored systems.
  • Pay annually instead of monthly.
  • Quote more than three insurers. This is the big one.
